## Frequently asked questions

#### What other parts do I need to buy?

That depends on the option you choose. The PCB-only option needs all other logger parts. The electronics kit also needs the 3D-printed enclosure parts, batteries, and an SD card. The complete hardware kit needs batteries and an SD card. All options will require you to purchase [sensors](/logger-build-guide/bodaqs-a8/sourcing-hardware/#sensors) separately. 

#### How much does postage cost?

Flat-rate postage by [Australia Post Standard International Service](https://auspost.com.au/parcels-mail/calculate-postage-delivery-times/#/option/international/AU/CA?fromPostcode=6076) to the US, Canada, EU and New Zealand is A$35. Postage within Australia is A$15. For other locations or services, <a href="mailto:info@bodaqs.net">email me</a>

#### Why don't you supply the batteries?

Shipping lithium batteries dramtically increases the chances of issues with shipping and/or customs, particularly internationally. Buying batteries locally is generally simpler, faster, and safer. Links to suitable battery suppliers in a range of international locations are shown [here](/logger-build-guide/bodaqs-a8/sourcing-hardware/#li-ion-battery)

#### Why don't you offer kits for the Prototype F logger?

Two reasons. First (as the name suggests) I don't consider it a finished design. I do intend to continue development of logger designs with a plug-in development board, but later ones will have some fairly significant design changes. Second, the Proto-F design doesn't have the PCB minimum order quantity issue of the A8: Proto-F PCBs are so cheap that ordering 5 of them is still very reasonable.

#### Is this cheaper than sourcing the parts myself?

Probably not, but it depends. On a per-unit basis sourcing parts yourself is the lowest-cost route, but it often doesn't quite work out that way due to  minimum order quantities (this is particularly the case for the [A8 PCB](/logger-build-guide/#prototype-f-not-dead-yet)) and disproportionate shipping costs for small orders. If you want to build 5 data loggers, then self sourcing is probably a better option. These options are for people who would rather save time, avoid supplier hunting, and receive a known-compatible set of parts.

#### What skills do I need for this project?

The kits reduce the sourcing work, but the logger is still a build-it-yourself project. You should be comfortable following the build guide, soldering and carrying out mechanical assembly. You will also need the ability to set up and install the logger on your bike: instructions are provided for this, but the level of hand-holding is less than you would get with, say, a new phone.

#### What kind of support will I get?

I will help as much as I reasonably can with questions about the supplied PCB or kit, assembly, and getting the logger working with the published guides. The BODAQS Discord community is also a good place to ask questions and share progress. I will endeavour to provide timely responses to queries but please understand that I am just one person, and help me out by reading the guides before you message me.

#### Will you build the logger for me?

Not currently. These options provide the PCB or parts needed to build your own logger; assembly remains part of the BODAQS build-it-yourself project.
I don't really have the time, and what time I do have I would rather put into improving the hardware and software. If your circumstances are exceptional we can discuss it.
